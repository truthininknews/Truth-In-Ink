AI in Education: Helpful Tool or Learning Threat?

Excessive AI use raises concerns about students' creativity, critical thinking, and reading comprehension, highlighting the need to balance technology with independent learning.

Keynote speaker and journalism coach Xydrone Cawaling discussed concerns about AI dependence, academic dishonesty, and students losing their authentic voices and unique identities.

Artificial intelligence (AI) is changing education, but excessive dependence on it raises concerns about students' memory, critical thinking, reading comprehension, and academic honesty.

Students who rely heavily on AI may struggle to understand lessons independently. Instead of developing their own ideas, they may simply copy generated answers, weakening their creativity and authentic voices.

AI can also encourage academic dishonesty when students use it to complete assignments without understanding the work. Over time, excessive dependence may limit opportunities to develop independent problem-solving skills.

Despite these concerns, AI can support learning by explaining difficult concepts, providing feedback, and helping students understand lessons in a judgment-free environment.

However, schools must ensure that AI use remains responsible. School boards should allocate funding for digital education, teacher training, and measures that protect students' personal information.

Teachers should encourage students to read textbooks, practice writing independently, and verify information instead of relying entirely on AI. These practices can help strengthen reading comprehension and critical thinking.

AI is a useful educational tool, but it cannot replace genuine learning. Students must balance technology with traditional learning methods to protect their creativity, knowledge, and academic growth.
