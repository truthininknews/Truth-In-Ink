BORROWED BRAINS

When Students Let AI Think for Them

As artificial intelligence makes schoolwork easier and faster, students risk sacrificing their originality, critical thinking, and independence for the convenience of instant answers.

In a world where answers are just one click away, students no longer have to spend hours thinking about difficult questions. Artificial Intelligence (AI) has made schoolwork faster and easier, but behind this convenience lies a growing concern: Are students learning, or are they simply letting technology do the work for them?

AI has become a common tool for students who struggle with assignments, essays, and lessons. It can explain difficult topics and help generate ideas. However, when students depend on it too much, they may lose opportunities to develop their own understanding. Instead of reading a lesson carefully or solving a problem independently, some students immediately turn to AI for answers.

The problem is not AI itself, but how students use it. When every answer is generated by a machine, students may become less confident in expressing their own thoughts. They might finish their assignments quickly without fully understanding what they have written. Eventually, completing schoolwork may become more important than learning from it.

Originality is another concern. Every student has different ideas, experiences, and ways of understanding the world. But when students repeatedly rely on AI-generated responses, their personal voices may become less noticeable in their work. If students allow technology to make every decision for them, how will they discover their own creativity and abilities?

Schools should not completely reject AI, because it can still support learning when used properly. Instead, teachers should encourage students to read before asking for summaries, develop their own answers before seeking assistance, and explain lessons in their own words. Students must also take responsibility for their education by using AI as a guide rather than a replacement for their efforts.

At the end of the day, education is not a competition to finish assignments first. It is a process of gaining knowledge, improving skills, and learning how to think independently. AI may provide answers, but students must make the effort to understand them.

Technology should help students become better thinkers, not turn them into followers of generated answers. The real measure of learning is not how quickly students complete their work, but how much they understand when the screen is turned off.
