TRUTH IN INK | FEATURE

REPORT CARDS IMPROVING, INSIGHT AND CURIOSITY DYING

As artificial intelligence reshapes education, guest speaker Xydrone Cawaling challenges student journalists to look beyond impressive report cards and question whether technology is strengthening understanding or replacing independent thought.

Speaking at the podium stands guest speaker Xydrone Cawaling, disputing the idea that progressive academics equal individual understanding.

While students' report cards and academic documents show outstanding results, Xydrone Cawaling stepped onto the podium, with countless student journalists gazing at him. Taking the stage at the Online Publication Press Conference, Xydrone Cawaling expressed his concern about the effects of artificial intelligence on education.

During the speech, Cawaling pointed out the flaws and abuses of what students can leverage with the assistance of AI. The speaker mentioned the benefits of what AI can do; however, the threat it poses could be a potential major blow to learners' independent comprehension.

Cawaling pointed out that having information doesn't automatically develop learners' intelligence, comprehension, and problem-solving skills. Disputing those aspects doesn't directly address those specific areas.

The speaker emphasized that obtaining information is distinct from inheriting knowledge, elaborating on the rift between gaining details and understanding something, especially in education. Cawaling warned student journalists of the adverse effects of depending on AI for learning opportunities.

Cawaling conveyed the potential downfall that could result from using AI without meticulous supervision. Throughout the speech, the speaker spoke about the risks and the major blow it could deliver nationally to students and future generations.

For Cawaling, seeing a huge jump in one's performance and academic results is outstanding progress. However, the speaker stated that not every improvement is earned with integrity. He also called on writers to encourage students to act honestly rather than deceive others about their academic performance.

As the speech continued, Cawaling's voice echoed through the minds of journalists beyond the Online Publication Press Conference podium, urging them to recognize that lack of supervision and illusions are small habits that should raise awareness.

When Xydrone Cawaling finished and stepped away from the podium, his message was evident and thought-provoking. The conference speech gave journalists motivation to take action on the rising issue before them. Cawaling prompted countless student journalists to look closer at what is overlooked, hold their pens, and strike for a better society and an education that can carry out its responsibilities to the next generation in the age of artificial intelligence.
