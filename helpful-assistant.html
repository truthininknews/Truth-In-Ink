AI: A Helpful Assistant or a Threat to Students’ Literacy?

Technology has made learning easier, faster, and more accessible than ever before. With just a few clicks, students can now obtain answers, generate essays, summarize reading materials, and complete academic tasks through Artificial Intelligence (AI). What once required hours of reading, researching, and writing can now be accomplished in minutes. While this advancement offers remarkable opportunities for education, it also raises an important question: At what point does the use of AI become a threat to students' literacy development?

There is no denying that AI has become a valuable companion in education. It helps students understand unfamiliar words, clarify difficult concepts, correct grammatical errors, and explore ideas beyond their textbooks. However, the problem begins when convenience replaces the learning process. Instead of making an effort to read and understand a passage, students ask AI to summarize it. Instead of organizing their thoughts and constructing meaningful sentences, they allow AI to write their essays. Instead of solving problems independently, they immediately seek answers from technology.

This growing dependence may create learners who know how to obtain answers but struggle to explain them. Reading, after all, is not simply recognizing words or finishing a paragraph. It involves comprehension, interpretation, analysis, and the ability to connect information with real-life experiences. Likewise, writing is not merely producing grammatically correct sentences; it is an expression of one's thoughts, creativity, reasoning, and understanding. When AI performs these intellectual tasks for students, opportunities to develop essential literacy skills are lost.

An even greater concern is the possibility of creating a generation of learners who are digitally dependent but intellectually passive. Students may become proficient in using technological tools without becoming proficient in understanding what they read or expressing what they think. They may submit impressive outputs but lack the confidence and ability to defend their ideas when asked to explain them. Such a situation challenges the very purpose of education, which is to develop knowledgeable, independent, and critical-minded individuals.

However, banning AI from classrooms is neither a practical nor a sustainable solution. Instead, schools must teach students how to use it responsibly and meaningfully. Teachers should encourage learners to read original materials before consulting AI-generated summaries, write their own reflections before seeking writing assistance, and critically examine whether AI-generated information is accurate and reliable. Most importantly, students must be reminded that technology is intended to support their learning, not replace their responsibility to learn.

AI should sharpen the minds of students, not become a substitute for them. Its purpose in education should be to strengthen human intelligence rather than weaken the skills that make learning meaningful.

As educators, we must remember that producing an essay is not necessarily evidence of literacy, just as obtaining the correct answer is not always proof of understanding. The true measure of learning lies in a student's ability to read with comprehension, write with purpose, think independently, and communicate with confidence.

Let us not allow the convenience of artificial intelligence to create an inconvenience for future generations: a generation that can ask almost anything but struggles to think, read, and write for itself.
