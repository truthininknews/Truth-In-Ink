GUILTY CONSCIENCE

As students increasingly rely on artificial intelligence for academic tasks and concerns over independent reading growth, the literacy crisis demands urgent action from schools, educators, and the government.

Head’s dropping, eyes closing, students staring at their assignments as the time ticks, some unable to answer, the other unable to read, long nights and many deadlines, pressure on academics and the weight of fatigue. Close by, phones open as Ai generates the answers.

Mr. Xydrone Cawaling, a cum laude graduate of CSU and a school paper adviser for five years, raised concerns about students’ reading abilities, claiming that 94% cannot read independently. This highlights the growing dependence on AI for academic tasks. AI must support, not replace, genuine learning. Education authorities must strengthen reading programs, while schools promote responsible AI use and independent thinking to ensure students develop essential literacy skills.

The recent use of AI in daily tasks may weaken the students' independent thinking and contribute to the gradual loss of literacy skills due to their dependence on Artificial Intelligence. When students' basic academic works turn into repetitive AI generated answers, causing students to submit similar essays that do not fit their writing style. This dependence limits the students' opportunities to practice reading, writing, and developing their creativity. Over time, students may have difficulty reading independently, expressing their thoughts, and distinguishing the wrong information generated by AI.

Using AI regularly has caused a massive shift in every student's basic thinking, reading comprehension, and originality that causes major problems to a students’ academics, worsening their learning capabilities, simultaneously, according to a study on AI dependency and students' comprehension skills, reliance on AI raises concerns about students' ability to understand and process information independently, this suggests that depending too much on AI may affect students' ability to understand what they read, think critically, and develop their own ideas, potentially affecting their academic performance and learning capabilities.

Despite knowing these effects students still continually use AI rather than depending on their own thinking. Ignoring the warnings that were stated on the AI , such as giving out wrong information, changing your writing style, and not always reliable sources. This shows that students may be relying too much on AI without fully understanding its limitations. As a result, they may struggle to identify incorrect information, maintain their own writing style, and evaluate the reliability of their sources, which can affect their critical thinking and independent learning.

To address the growing dependence on Artificial Intelligence and the decline in students’ reading abilities, schools must strengthen reading programs, organize activities that develop independent thinking, and encourage students to practice reading and writing without relying entirely on AI. Teachers should guide students in using AI responsibly as a learning tool rather than a replacement for their own ideas and efforts. Furthermore, the Department of Education and the government must support literacy initiatives and provide students with opportunities to improve their comprehension skills. Through these efforts, students can develop stronger reading abilities, critical thinking, and originality while using technology responsibly.

The time to act is now. Schools, teachers, parents, and the government must work together to address the growing dependence on AI and protect students’ literacy skills. Students must take responsibility for their own learning by practicing reading, writing, and critical thinking instead of relying on AI for every academic task. Remember, AI should be a tool for learning, not a replacement for it. Let us put down our dependence on artificial intelligence and pick up the habit of independent learning before we lose the skills that truly matter.
