TRUTH IN INK | EDITORIAL

GUILTY CONSCIENCE

As students increasingly rely on artificial intelligence for academic tasks and concerns over independent reading growth, the literacy crisis demands urgent action from schools, educators, and the government.

Heads dropping, eyes closing, students staring at their assignments as time ticks away. Some are unable to answer, while others struggle to read. Long nights, countless deadlines, academic pressure, and fatigue weigh heavily on students. Nearby, phones remain open as AI generates the answers.

Mr. Xydrone Cawaling, a cum laude graduate of CSU and a school paper adviser for five years, raised concerns about students' reading abilities, claiming that 94% cannot read independently. This highlights the growing dependence on AI for academic tasks. AI must support, not replace, genuine learning. Education authorities must strengthen reading programs, while schools promote responsible AI use and independent thinking to ensure students develop essential literacy skills.

The increasing use of AI in daily academic tasks may weaken students' independent thinking and contribute to the gradual loss of literacy skills. When basic schoolwork becomes a series of AI-generated answers, students may submit similar essays that do not reflect their own writing styles. This dependence limits their opportunities to practice reading, writing, and creativity. Over time, students may struggle to read independently, express their thoughts, and identify incorrect information generated by AI.

Regular AI use may also affect students' thinking, reading comprehension, and originality, creating challenges in their academic development. Research on AI dependency and students' comprehension skills raises concerns about their ability to understand and process information independently. This suggests that excessive reliance on AI may affect students' ability to understand what they read, think critically, and develop their own ideas, potentially affecting their academic performance and learning.

Despite these concerns, some students continue to use AI instead of relying on their own thinking. They may overlook its limitations, including inaccurate information, changes to their writing style, and unreliable sources. This shows how students can become overly dependent on AI without fully understanding its weaknesses. As a result, they may struggle to identify incorrect information, maintain their own writing style, and evaluate the reliability of sources, affecting their critical thinking and independent learning.

To address the growing dependence on AI and concerns about students' reading abilities, schools must strengthen reading programs, organize activities that develop independent thinking, and encourage students to practice reading and writing without relying entirely on AI. Teachers should guide students in using AI responsibly as a learning tool rather than a replacement for their own ideas and efforts. Furthermore, the Department of Education and the government must support literacy initiatives and provide opportunities for students to improve their comprehension skills. Through these efforts, students can develop stronger reading abilities, critical thinking, and originality while using technology responsibly.

The time to act is now. Schools, teachers, parents, and the government must work together to address the growing dependence on AI and protect students' literacy skills. Students must take responsibility for their own learning by practicing reading, writing, and critical thinking instead of relying on AI for every academic task. Remember, AI should be a tool for learning, not a replacement for it. Let us put down our dependence on artificial intelligence and pick up the habit of independent learning before we lose the skills that truly matter.
